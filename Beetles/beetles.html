beetles

External morphology

Head

Beetles have hard heads with adaptable eyes that vary by species and activity, sometimes including simple eyes called ocelli.

Mouthparts

Beetles have strong, pincer-like mandibles for grasping and cutting food, with smaller mouthparts that help move food, and males often have much larger mandibles than females.

Thorax

The thorax is segmented into the two discernible parts.

Prothorax and Pterothorax

Prothorax and the pterothorax hold the legs and wings. From above, beetles seem to have three sections, but the middle part is a hard plate called the pronotum, and the rest of the thorax is hidden under the wings.

Legs

Beetles have segmented legs ending in small tarsi with claws, mainly used for walking but adapted for swimming, digging, or jumping in some species.

Wings

Beetles' front wings form hard covers called elytra that protect the hindwings used for flight. Some beetles have soft or fused elytra, and many species cannot fly even though they have wings.

Abdomen

A beetle's abdomen lies behind the thorax and is made of ring-like segments with breathing holes called spiracles. It includes three main parts—the soft tergum, the small pleura, and the harder, visible sternum—and usually has no appendages.
